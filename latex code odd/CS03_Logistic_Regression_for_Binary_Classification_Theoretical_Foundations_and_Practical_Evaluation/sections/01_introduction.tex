\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Importance and Relevance to Machine Learning and Society}
The continuous expansion of data-intensive systems across healthcare, finance, cyber-infrastructure, and autonomous engineering has elevated statistical machine learning from theoretical interest to an indispensable societal pillar \cite{hastie2009}. Modern computational environments frequently operate within high-dimensional regimes where the number of measurement parameters $p$ rivals or drastically exceeds the number of empirical observations $n$. Under such conditions, classical unconstrained parameter estimation methods fail due to mathematical singularity, multicollinearity, and catastrophic overfitting.

In this case study, we investigate \textbf{Logistic Regression for Binary Classification: Theoretical Foundations and Practical Evaluation}, situated within the operational reality of clinical emergency triage and credit default underwriting requiring calibrated posterior probabilities and optimal decision thresholds under asymmetric misclassification costs. When machine learning models are deployed in safety-critical domains---such as diagnosing cardiovascular conditions, establishing automated loan risk, or predicting material degradation---model generalization, numerical stability, and bias-variance equilibrium are imperative \cite{breiman2001}. Unstable models exhibiting wild swings under minor sample permutations cannot be audited, verified, or ethically entrusted with life-altering determinations.

\subsection{Objectives of the Case Study Analysis}
The primary engineering and analytical objectives of this investigation are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item To establish the rigorous mathematical formulation governing parameter estimation, loss minimization, and regularization mechanisms within the framework of Logistic Regression for Binary Classification: Theoretical Foundations and Practical Evaluation.
  \item To analyze the operational bottlenecks associated with Non-linear log-odds mapping, non-existence of closed-form solution requiring iterative optimization (IRLS), sensitivity to separation in high dimensions, and optimal threshold selection under unequal class distributions., demonstrating failure modes of unconstrained baselines.
  \item To conduct extensive empirical validation across benchmark datasets, comparing training, cross-validation, and out-of-sample generalization performance.
  \item To examine the trade-offs between computational complexity, predictive accuracy, and interpretability using standardized statistical metrics.
  \item To map the technical outcomes directly to ABET Program Outcomes and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, establishing societal relevance.
\end{itemize}
