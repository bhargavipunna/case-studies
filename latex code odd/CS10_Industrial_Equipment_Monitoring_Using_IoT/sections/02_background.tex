\section{Theoretical Background and IoT Protocol Foundations}

\subsection{Sensor Transduction and Signal Conditioning}
At the foundational perception layer, physical phenomena are transduced into calibrated electrical potentials. The governing mathematical transduction relationship is characterized by:
\begin{equation}
  X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n] e^{-j 2\pi k n / N}, \quad \text{BPFO} = \frac{N_b}{2} f_r \left(1 - \frac{d_b}{d_p} \cos \phi\right)
\label{eq:core_math}
\end{equation}
To suppress high-frequency thermal noise and 50/60 Hz electromagnetic hum, raw analog signals undergo active analog filtering followed by oversampled analog-to-digital conversion (ADC). Digital low-pass Butterworth filters and finite impulse response (FIR) moving-average windows are executed at the micro-controller register level prior to packet encapsulation.

At the application layer, lightweight publish-subscribe protocols such as Message Queuing Telemetry Transport (MQTT) and Constrained Application Protocol (CoAP) minimize transport overhead. MQTT establishes persistent TCP connections with a minimalist 2-byte header, offering Quality of Service (QoS 0, 1, 2) delivery guarantees over unreliable links \cite{bahga2014internet}.

\subsection{Edge Computing and Energy Harvesting Architecture}
To extend remote node lifespans to multiple years, edge devices cycle aggressively between active sensing states and deep-sleep quiescent regimes. The average current consumption $I_{\text{avg}}$ over an operational cycle $T_{\text{cycle}}$ is derived as:
\begin{equation}
  I_{\text{avg}} = \frac{I_{\text{active}} \cdot T_{\text{active}} + I_{\text{sleep}} \cdot (T_{\text{cycle}} - T_{\text{active}})}{T_{\text{cycle}}}
\end{equation}
By constraining active transmission bursts to $T_{\text{active}} \le 150\text{ ms}$ and optimizing sleep current $I_{\text{sleep}} < 15\,\mu\text{A}$, battery life exceeds 36 months on standard lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) storage cells supplemented with miniature photovoltaic energy harvesting.
